# Safety and clinical-use boundary

DeliriumWatch is a software research prototype. It is not a medical device and has not been clinically validated.

- Do not display or describe the research change index as a diagnosis, probability, or clinical alarm.
- Do not delay, initiate, stop, or alter clinical care based on this system.
- Use validated delirium assessments and clinical review as the reference standard in research.
- Skin temperature from the watch is peripheral/object temperature, not core body temperature.
- Wrist movement is not equivalent to sleep, posture, agitation, delirium subtype, or functional status.
- Failure to complete a cognitive task can be informative in research but is highly nonspecific: sleep, procedures, hearing/vision issues, pain, motor impairment, device non-wear and simple refusal can all cause missing responses.
- Raw PPG and accelerometer data can be identifying/health-sensitive research data. Use pseudonymous IDs, access controls, retention rules and ethics/privacy review.
- Researchers should define procedures for consent/capacity, withdrawal, adverse events, device hygiene, skin irritation, charging, falls/entanglement risks, and management of participants who become distressed by prompts.

The code deliberately calls its combined score an **acute change index**, not a delirium or sepsis risk score. A validated clinical model should be added only after an appropriate prospective study with locked analysis, external validation and governance.
